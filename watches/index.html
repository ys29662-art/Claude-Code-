The Maison

One hundred years at the same bench.

In 1924, Auguste Aurélien set a single condition for his atelier: no watch would leave it that he had not finished by his own hand. Four generations later, that condition still holds.

We make fewer than four hundred timepieces a year. Not by limitation — by choice. A movement that takes nine months to finish cannot be hurried, and we have never tried.

Savoir-Faire

The hand before the machine.

Every surface a customer will never see is finished as though they will.

01 — Anglage

Hand-bevelled bridges

Each interior angle is filed and polished by hand against a rotating tin disc — a technique no machine has yet replicated for the sharp inward corners.

02 — Guilloché

Dials cut on a rose engine

Our dials are engraved on a 19th-century straight-line engine, turned entirely by the watchmaker's own steady hand. One slip ends the dial.

03 — Réglage

Timed across six positions

Every calibre is adjusted and re-adjusted over fourteen days until it holds chronometric accuracy in all positions and temperatures.
